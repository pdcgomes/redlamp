# The brief, the concept and the design doc

The producer opens the job with a design doc at `docs/plans/<date>-<slug>-promo.md`. It holds everything decided about the promo, in this order, and the canvas links it. The star promo's ([2026-10-06-star-promo.md](../../../docs/plans/2026-10-06-star-promo.md)) is a complete example.

## The brief

A table, every row filled in before any concept work:

| Row | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Goal | The one outcome, measurable: stars, downloads, visits to a page, sign-ups. With today's number. |
| Audience | Who, and what they already know. Redlamp's are photographers on the Mac who know Lightroom, and the developers and open-source followers who hand out GitHub stars. |
| Platforms | Which feeds, so which shapes: 9:16 (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, Stories), 1:1 or 4:5 (Instagram and Facebook feeds, LinkedIn, X), 16:9 (YouTube, X, Reddit). |
| Length | In seconds and in bars of the grid. 7 to 20 seconds for an ask. |
| The one message | One sentence. If it needs two, it's two promos. |
| The ask | What the viewer should do, and where: an address short enough to type from a phone. |
| Tone | How it should feel, within the brand: playful, calm, cinematic. |
| Must keep | The brand's rules, claims only from the README, anything the owner has said. |
| Success | How the goal will be read afterwards, and when. |

## The concept

One to three concepts, a paragraph each: what happens, why it would hold someone for its length, and how it ends on the ask. Good concepts for Redlamp so far are small physical stories with the brand's own objects: the lamp, its light, the paper, the darkroom. The director picks one and logs the choice and the reason in the canvas's decisions.

A short promo is usually a gag in four beats:

1. **Tension**: something is about to happen (the lamp charges, a print is about to come up).
2. **Release**: it happens, on the drop.
3. **Payoff**: the consequence, which carries the message (the badge lights, the sign drops).
4. **The ask, plainly**: what to do and where, held long enough to read.

## The design doc's sections

1. **Brief**, the table above.
2. **Concept**, the chosen one, in a few numbered beats.
3. **Hooks**, five, with an ID each ([copy.md](copy.md)).
4. **Script**, the beat sheet: beat, time, picture, words, sound ([script.md](script.md)).
5. **Formats**, each shape and how its layout differs, with the safe zones.
6. **Sound**, the score's key, tempo, arrangement, and the sounds on each event.
7. **Post copy**, for each platform, and the alt text.
8. **For the owner to decide**, the open questions, each mirrored as a Needs you item.
9. **Results**, added a week after posting: the goal's number then and now, and what the platforms report.
