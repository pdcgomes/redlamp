# Redlamp: open-source models for object removal and distraction detection

A licensing survey of models Redlamp (MPL-2.0; macOS now, iPhone later) could ship bundled or download on demand and run on Apple silicon, for (a) inpainting / object removal and (b) open-vocabulary detection and segmentation of distracting objects.

Prepared 3 October 2026. Every licence statement below was read from a primary source on that date: the licence file in the code repository (via the GitHub licence API and raw files), the Hugging Face model card and its metadata (`https://huggingface.co/api/models/<id>`), the dataset's own terms page, or the data section of the paper. Links are inline in the tables and collected in §5. Two pages (Places2) were only reachable through the Internet Archive; the snapshots are linked. This is an engineering survey, not legal advice.

**Verdict legend**

- **Shippable**: the code and weights licences allow commercial use and redistribution, nothing has to be imposed on Redlamp's users, and no training-data source is known to carry non-commercial or research-only terms. "Data undisclosed" is noted separately: undisclosed is not the same as clean.
- **Problematic**: commercial use is allowed, but something needs the owner's or counsel's judgement: use restrictions that must flow down to users, revenue or user caps, research-only datasets in the training lineage, sanctions, or unclear provenance.
- **Not allowed**: the code or weights licence is non-commercial or research-only, there is no licence at all, the licence excludes territories Redlamp ships to, or (by the owner's rule) the model is trained on Places2.

**Why flow-down licences are "Problematic".** CreativeML OpenRAIL-M says the Attachment A use restrictions "MUST be included as an enforceable provision by You in any type of legal agreement (e.g. a license) governing the use and/or distribution of the Model or Derivatives of the Model", and "You shall require all of Your users who use the Model or a Derivative of the Model to comply" ([licence text](https://github.com/Fantasy-Studio/Paint-by-Example/blob/main/LICENSE), §4 and §5). MPL-2.0 can't carry field-of-use restrictions, so the weights would have to ship under their own EULA-style terms, and Redlamp-with-the-model would no longer be usable for any purpose. The same structure appears in the Gemma Terms of Use (§3.1: "include the use restrictions ... as an enforceable provision", [terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms)), the Kolors model licence (§3a), the Llama 3.1 licence (Acceptable Use Policy and "Built with Llama"), and Meta's SAM and DINOv3 licences ("you may only do so under the terms of this Agreement", plus military and ITAR bans). Google now licenses Gemma 4 under Apache-2.0 ([page](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/apache_2)), but the Gemma 2 encoders used by Sana and Lumina stay under the Gemma Terms.

## 1. Summary

- **No small, clean, pretrained inpainter exists yet.** Every LaMa/MI-GAN-class inpainter checked is trained on Places2, whose terms say "You will use the data only for non-commercial research and educational purposes. You will NOT distribute the above images" ([2021 snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20210220220343/http://places2.csail.mit.edu/download.html)): Big-LaMa, MI-GAN, MAT, Co-Mod-GAN, FcF, ZITS/ZITS++, MISF and CompVis LDM. The 2025 PixelHacker pretrains on Objects365 instead, but that dataset is "available for the academic purpose only" and half its images come from Places365. No LaMa retrain on public-domain data, and no inpainter trained on PD12M or Megalith-10M, was found. The earlier research checks out on every point (Places2; NVIDIA non-commercial code in Co-Mod-GAN and FcF; CC BY-NC MAT; LaMa's ADE20K-trained perceptual loss).
- **MI-GAN's weights are now MIT.** Picsart added `LICENSE-WEIGHTS` (MIT) on 14 Sep 2026. The only remaining obstacles are the Places2 training data and some NVIDIA-copyright training utilities that inference doesn't use. At 5.9M parameters (~16 MB fp16; ~6 ms per 512² image on a Pixel 8a GPU, 22 ms via WebGPU on an M4 Max) it's the best architecture to retrain on clean data.
- **The best shippable generative option is FLUX.2 [klein] 4B** (Black Forest Labs, Jan 2026). Its weights, VAE and Qwen3-4B text encoder are all Apache-2.0, and the repository isn't gated. diffusers has a mask-based `Flux2KleinInpaintPipeline`, and MLX ports exist (4.6 GB at 4-bit; mflux supports it). The caveats: training data is undisclosed, and with the text encoder it's ~8B parameters, so it only makes sense as an opt-in download on Macs with 16 GB or more, not on iPhone. The 9B klein and FLUX.2 [dev] are non-commercial.
- **Other Apache/MIT editors are licence-OK but too big for on-device use:** Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (20B plus a 7B encoder), Step1X-Edit v1p2 (~12B plus 7B; the paper doesn't name the text-to-image model it started from), BAGEL (14B), LongCat-Image-Edit (~6B plus 7B), Z-Image (6B plus 4B), Flex.2-preview (8B) and Chroma1-HD (8.9B). Watch for terms hidden in components: OmniGen2 is labelled Apache-2.0 but bundles Qwen2.5-VL-3B (Qwen Research Licence, non-commercial); the new Qwen-Image-2.1 (Sep 2026) moved to the Qwen Research Licence; HiDream-E1 needs Llama 3.1.
- **The earlier diffusion verdicts hold.** FLUX.1 Fill and Kontext [dev] are non-commercial. SD 3.5 is free only below US$1M annual revenue and adds an Acceptable Use Policy (red flag). SD 1.5/2/SDXL inpainting and everything built on them (PowerPaint, BrushNet, InstructPix2Pix, Paint-by-Example, ObjectClear's SDXL base) carry OpenRAIL or worse. Two new flags: HunyuanImage excludes the EU, the UK and South Korea, and Kandinsky 2.2 inpainting is Apache-2.0 but published by Sber AI, whose parent Sberbank is under OFAC full blocking sanctions (a question for counsel).
- **For segmentation, SAM 2.1 remains the clean anchor.** Code and checkpoints are Apache-2.0, and Meta trained the released model on SA-V (CC BY 4.0), its own licensed internal video and SA-1B. For iPhone, Meta's EdgeTAM (Apache-2.0) runs at 16 FPS on an iPhone 15 Pro Max and has an official Core ML export. One caveat applies to this whole family: SAM 2's image encoder is initialised from MAE-pretrained Hiera, and Hiera's public checkpoints are ImageNet-1K pretrains.
- **SAM 3 and 3.1 allow commercial use but are Problematic.** The SAM License has no non-commercial clause or revenue cap and allows redistribution, but only under the SAM License itself. It also bans military, nuclear, espionage and weapons uses, forbids reverse engineering, lets Meta change the terms at any time, and requires indemnity. The Hugging Face weights are gated behind manual approval. The "Apache-2.0" distillations (EfficientSAM3, SAM3-LiteText) keep SAM 3 components or lineage, so they don't escape it.
- **Open-vocabulary detectors have permissive weights but research-only data in their lineage.** Florence-2 (MIT; 0.23B/0.77B; Core ML and MLX ports), OWLv2 (Apache-2.0; 155M), Grounding DINO, MM-Grounding-DINO, LLMDet and OmDet-Turbo (Apache-2.0; 115–233M) and the new Falcon Perception (Apache-2.0; 0.6B, with masks; official MLX backend) all train on Objects365, ImageNet-22k, Flickr30k or similar, or distil from DINOv3 or SAM 3. Moondream 2 (Apache-2.0, 1.9B) and Qwen3-VL-2B/4B (Apache-2.0) show no research-only data only because their data isn't disclosed at all. Applied strictly, the Places2 rule leaves no open-vocabulary detector standing, so the owner needs a policy for discriminative models, whose outputs are boxes and masks rather than pixels.
- **Not allowed for detection:** YOLO-World (GPL-3.0); Ultralytics YOLO, YOLO-World and YOLOE (AGPL-3.0, or a paid licence); EdgeSAM (S-Lab, non-commercial); LocateAnything-3B (NVIDIA, non-commercial); MetaCLIP (CC BY-NC); Qwen2.5-VL-3B (Qwen Research Licence); and Apple's MobileCLIP, FastVLM, DFN-CLIP and AIMv2 (Apple's research-only `apple-amlr` licence). Apple publishes no open inpainting model, and its MGIE editing weights are CC BY-NC.
- **Suggested path.** Ship OpenCV's classical inpainting (Apache-2.0, no training data) as the always-available fallback. If the owner accepts undisclosed training data, offer FLUX.2 [klein] 4B as an opt-in Mac download. For the default on-device eraser, train a LaMa-class model on Megalith-10M and PD12M (plus SA-V video frames for object masks) and distil it into an MI-GAN-size model; Big-LaMa took ~240 hours on 8×V100. For distractions, either accept Florence-2/OWLv2-class lineage, or fine-tune an Apache-2.0 closed-set detector (RF-DETR S/M, ~32M) on Open Images V7 and keep SAM 2.1 for masks. Open Images V7 images are listed as CC BY 2.0 and its annotations are CC BY 4.0; it has Person, Car, Bottle, Plastic bag, Waste container, Traffic sign, Street light and Billboard classes, but no power-line class. RF-DETR's published checkpoints were themselves pretrained on Objects365, so a clean version means training from its Apache-2.0 DINOv2 backbone.

## 4. Training our own

### 4.1 Recommended plan

1. **Model.** Retrain a LaMa-class FFC generator with the Apache-2.0 [LaMa code](https://github.com/advimman/lama) on clean data, then distil it into an MI-GAN-size student for real-time and iPhone use. For scale: Big-LaMa has 51M parameters and was trained on 4.5M images for "approximately 240 hours" on eight V100s ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07161)). MI-GAN (MIT) learns by knowledge distillation from a larger teacher with adversarial and R1 losses and no ImageNet perceptual network ([config](https://github.com/Picsart-AI-Research/MI-GAN/blob/main/configs/experiment/migan_places512.yaml), [loss](https://github.com/Picsart-AI-Research/MI-GAN/blob/main/lib/experiments/loss.py)), so our clean LaMa can be its teacher. Re-implement the NVIDIA ops its training model imports (`upfirdn2d`, `conv2d_resample`, `bias_act`) in plain PyTorch, as its MIT inference module already does, and rewrite the NVIDIA-headed `loss.py`.
2. **Data.** Use Megalith-10M (real-world photos, the closest match to Redlamp's inputs) plus the photographic part of PD12M (§4.2). Real object-removal pairs can come from SA-V (CC BY 4.0) videos: when a masked object moves, frames where the background behind it is visible give ground truth. That's an idea to prototype, not a tested method.
3. **Masks.** Use LaMa's procedural mask generator (Apache-2.0), plus object-shaped masks from SAM 2.1 automatic mask generation on the clean pool, SA-V masklets, or Open Images V7 masks (CC BY 4.0). Avoid masks from SA-1B (research-only), COCO and ADE20K, and the [NVIDIA Irregular Mask Dataset](https://nv-adlr.github.io/publication/partialconv-inpainting), whose page states no licence.
4. **Optional diffusion route.** Train a small latent-diffusion inpainter from scratch on the same data. No text encoder is needed for removal (unconditional, or a learned null token), which sidesteps the Gemma, Llama and Qwen-Research traps. For a VAE, use [FLUX.2-VAE](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-VAE) (Apache-2.0), [SDXL VAE](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/sdxl-vae) (MIT) or [TAESD](https://huggingface.co/madebyollin/taesd) (MIT), whose training data is undisclosed, or train our own. For code, use [diffusers](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers) (Apache-2.0) or [SiT](https://github.com/willisma/SiT) (MIT); avoid copying [facebookresearch/DiT](https://github.com/facebookresearch/DiT/blob/main/LICENSE.txt), which is CC BY-NC 4.0.
5. **Bases for an inpainting fine-tune** whose data is mostly openly licensed:
   - [CommonArt β](https://huggingface.co/aipicasso/commonart-beta) (Apache-2.0; PixArt-Σ architecture; trained on CommonCatalog CC-BY, Megalith-10M, Smithsonian Open Access and ArtBench CC0; calm2-7b encoder, [Apache-2.0](https://huggingface.co/cyberagent/calm2-7b); SDXL VAE, MIT).
   - [CommonCanvas S-C / XL-C](https://huggingface.co/common-canvas/CommonCanvas-S-C) (weights CC BY-SA 4.0; UNet trained on CC-BY and CC-BY-SA YFCC images). It reuses SD2's VAE and an SD2 (OpenCLIP, LAION-trained) text encoder ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16825), [encoder config](https://huggingface.co/common-canvas/CommonCanvas-S-C/blob/main/text_encoder/config.json)), so LAION lineage remains.
6. **Synthetic pairs from a permissive teacher.** Apache-2.0 editors (FLUX.2 [klein] 4B, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511) put no restrictions on their outputs, so they can generate removal pairs for a small student, though their undisclosed data remains a residual question. Don't use FLUX.1 [dev] outputs: its licence says "You may not use the Output to train, fine-tune or distill a model that is competitive". Llama-derived teachers force "Llama" into the student's name.

### 4.3 Licence traps

- **Perceptual losses.** LaMa's `resnet_pl` is an ADE20K-trained, ImageNet-initialised ResNet-50 (non-commercial terms twice over). VGG-16/19 perceptual and style losses, LPIPS backbones, and Projected-GAN-style pretrained discriminators all come from ImageNet. Replacements: [DINOv2](https://huggingface.co/facebook/dinov2-base) (Apache-2.0), SigLIP 2 (Apache-2.0), SAM 2.1's Hiera encoder (Apache-2.0, ImageNet MAE lineage), or a feature network we self-train on the clean pool. Avoid DINOv3 (its licence flows down like SAM 3's). FID and Inception metrics used only for evaluation don't end up in the shipped weights.
- **NVIDIA StyleGAN code.** StyleGAN2(-ADA) ops sit inside Co-Mod-GAN, FcF, MAT and MI-GAN's training path; the [StyleGAN2-ADA licence](https://github.com/NVlabs/stylegan2-ada-pytorch/blob/main/LICENSE.txt) limits use to "research or evaluation purposes only".
- **Places contamination.** Places2 data also reaches models through Objects365 (half Places365) and through any model pretrained on Objects365.
- **Relabelled licences.** Many Hugging Face repos tag derivatives as MIT or Apache-2.0 when the base is OpenRAIL, non-commercial or SAM-licensed (InstructPix2Pix, PowerPaint, EfficientSAM3, OmniGen2). Always trace `base_model` and the bundled components.
- **Text encoders.** Gemma 2 (Sana, Lumina, PRX), Llama 3.1 (HiDream), ChatGLM3/GLM-4 (Kolors, CogView4; registration required) and Qwen Research (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen-Image-2.1) each bring their own terms.
- **Attribution.** For CC BY and CC BY-SA training data, whether a model counts as an "adaptation" is unsettled; publish a training-data attribution list.
